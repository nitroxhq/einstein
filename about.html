Why I built einstein.

A grade portal can tell you your average dropped. It can't tell you why, what it cost you, or what you need next. I wanted the opposite of that.

01 — the problem

Grades move. Portals don't explain.

Checking a grade is usually an anxious guess. The number changed, but the reason stayed hidden behind a syllabus PDF and a weighted formula.

The sudden drop

An assignment posts and your average slips from 89.4% to 86.8%. The portal shows the new number and nothing else — no category, no leverage, no cause.

opacity

Weights are hidden math

Exams at 45%, projects at 25%, quizzes at 15%, homework at 10%, participation at 5% — plus dropped scores and curves. The formula decides your grade, but you never see it work.

complexity

“What do I need on the final?”

So you open an ad-ridden calculator, retype your syllabus, and hope it guessed the same rules your teacher did.

guesswork

Your data isn't yours

Canvas, PowerSchool, and Blackboard are built for administrators. Your performance lives in their database, on their terms.

ownership

02 — why I made it

I wanted the explanation, not the verdict.

Einstein started with a simple frustration: a grade should come with a reason. If a score moved, you should be able to see which assignment moved it, how much the weight amplified it, and what that means for the next one.

I also didn't want another account, another server, or another dashboard that treats a student's data as inventory. So Einstein is file-first: the app is the interface to your data, not the owner of it. Everything is readable JSON and Markdown, stored locally, and it works offline.

03 — what it does

Three ideas, one grade assistant.

The same three tenets that shape every part of the app.

The Delta Engine

Every new score gets an instant autopsy: which category shifted, how much leverage it had, and whether your trajectory is up or down. The math is documented in docs/architecture.md.

delta calculation

The What-If Lab

Enter hypothetical scores, set a target, and see the exact minimum you need across what's left. Drop-lowest rules update in real time. Try the preview.

simulation

File-first sovereignty

Course data, grade history, and simulations live in human-readable files on your device. No proprietary database, no remote server, no telemetry. The schema is in docs/data-schema.md.

local storage

04 — who it's for

Anyone whose grade is decided by a formula.

High school, undergraduate, and graduate students with weighted syllabi, dropped scores, curved exams, and finals that carry too much weight. If a portal gives you a number and no explanation, Einstein is the missing second half.

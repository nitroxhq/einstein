02 — app comparison

The same grade, four very different tools.

A deeper cut of the README comparison: what each kind of tool actually does, where it stops being useful, and which one answers the question you're asking at 11pm.

standard lms

Canvas, Blackboard, PowerSchool

Holds the official gradebook. It is the source of truth because your teacher types the scores there.

Wins at
Being authoritative. Every other tool is a copy of it.
Breaks down at
Explaining itself. A zero for something you haven't turned in lands in the total like a real score, and category weights stay opaque.

Trust its numbers, not its narrative.

ad-hoc calculators

RogerHub, Excel sheets

One formula for one question: what do I need on the final to keep my A?

Wins at
Speed. No account, no sync, no permission — type numbers, get an answer.
Breaks down at
Anything past a single course and a single term: no history, no drop rules, no GPA picture.

Great at the one question, blind to the semester around it.

other grade apps

GradeWay, Gradiate

Mobile apps that pull grades from a supported portal and layer GPA goals and alerts on top.

Wins at
Polished mobile UI, notifications, and zero manual entry once a portal is linked.
Breaks down at
Portal dependence (HAC, PowerSchool), closed data, and what-ifs that inherit the portal's own category math.

Convenient if your school is on the list. Einstein doesn't need a list.

einstein

Explain first, then simulate

Reads course files you own, decomposes every grade change, and models the semester split explicitly — categories weighted inside the coursework share, the final weighted on its own.

Wins at
Saying why a number moved, and exactly what the next one has to be.
Breaks down at
It's early: there's no portal sync yet, so a course starts as a file you write or import.

Different trade: less automation, more explanation, and the data stays yours.
